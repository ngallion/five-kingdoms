# The Fairground — Running the Wayfair at the Table

**Type:** GM kit — games, performers, food, prizes, and scene tools for any night at the fair
**Use with:** [The Wayfair](the-wayfair.md) *(lore, the Loosening, GM truths)* · [The Stutter](../../quests/active/the-stutter.md) *(Session 1 stages here)*
**One rule shapes everything:** no act, game, or dish uses one aspect alone — the fair's only law. Every stall is a *pairing*, which is why the whole fairground is a Loosening engine hiding in plain sight.

## The shape of a fair night

1. **Lamplight** — the lamp-mender walks the grounds at dusk touching each lantern alight in sequence; fairgoers follow him in a slow parade nobody organized.
2. **The open hours** — games, food, small stages, the wonder-works. This is where the party wanders, meets, and plays.
3. **The supper** — long tables, all comers, pay-what-you-carry. Strangers are seated *interleaved*, never in their own groups; it's the one fair custom with teeth.
4. **The headline act** — the big stage. On most circuits, the Myriad. This solstice, a dark stage and a thin excuse.
5. **The lullaby** — the closing song, every night, every kingdom. The crowd hums it home and has lost it by morning.

Frame scenes to this clock and the night runs itself; the skip in [the Stutter](../../quests/active/the-stutter.md) lands hardest mid-act 4, when the fair is at full song.

## Fair games *(playable with real dice)*

All games use the players' actual duality dice — no character stats unless noted; these are games of luck, nerve, and showmanship, and a PC's flair matters more than their modifier. House custom: winning graciously or losing loudly both please the crowd. Once per night, if a PC plays a game *with* someone (not against — the fair notices the difference), the GM may hand out a Hope on the spot ([Braid guidance](../magic.md#at-the-daggerheart-table)).

- **Hammer & Feather** *(Forgeholm iron + Luminar grace)* — swing the great hammer to ring the bell, but a feather sits atop the striker: ring it *without* the feather leaving its cup. **Roll both duality dice: 10+ on either rings the bell (small prize); doubles of any value "makes the hammer sing" — bell rung, feather unstirred, choose from the shelf.** Barker: *"Strength is easy, friend. Strength with manners wins the shelf!"*
- **The Weigher's Wager** *(Sophara's scales + Agoran's nerve)* — three iron cups, one weighted; the barker shuffles slow, in plain sight, narrating philosophy the whole time. The trick: he *will* tell you which cup, truthfully, if asked directly — but you must call your cup before his count of three, and the count starts the moment you step up. **Roll Instinct (or just pick a cup): the game is really about whether the player commits.** Freezing forfeits the stake. Sopharans lose constantly and keep coming back; watch one almost-call and walk away shaking. *(That's a [canary](../anchors-and-engines.md#gm-notes), staged as a carnival game.)*
- **The Yoke Run** *(Agoran's teamwork + Forgeholm's timing)* — two players, one yoke, two full pails, a plank course. Both cross dry or neither wins. **Each player rolls a duality die; if both roll 7+, clean run — but if the two results differ by more than 4, someone rushed: both get soaked (crowd delight, no prize, free towels).** The only game with no solo version. Barker: *"No heroes on the plank!"*
- **Theora's Tray** *(Veritas's eye + Luminar's misdirection)* — twelve oddments on a tray, ten seconds, then the cloth: name what's *missing*. The barker's patter is engineered distraction. **Roll Knowledge 12+ — or the player can genuinely memorize a real list you read once, fast; honor whichever they choose.** Triple prize if they also name what was *added*, which nobody thinks to check.
- **The Sixth Ring** *(pairing: nobody can say)* — a ring-toss with six pegs. Five pay prizes. The sixth stands apart, unpainted, and pays nothing; every Wayfarer will tell you a different story about why it's there, and every stall on every circuit has one. **Landing it: 11+ on a single duality die at disadvantage-ish distance.** When someone rings it, the barker goes quiet, touches the peg once, and the crowd — without knowing why — buys the thrower drinks all night. *(GM: the guest's peg. File under [the sixth wagon](the-wayfair.md#how-they-operate). If a PC rings it, note who — it may matter to you later.)*

## Performers *(the small stages — names renameable)*

- **The Myriad** — [Tam Merrival](../../characters/npcs/tam-merrival.md), the closing act. *Dark this solstice.* The stagehands re-rig his empty stage every night anyway; ask a player to notice.
- **Seralind's Mirror** *(Luminar paint + Sophara's question)* — a fire-painter who takes a shouted question from the crowd and paints the answer in burning pigment; the image lasts until the flames die. One painting this solstice *didn't fade until dawn*, and she has been sitting with it, not talking. *(Loosening surging — a discoverable.)*
- **The Fact** *(Veritas proof + Agoran roar)* — a barrel-chested lecturer who proves one true, astonishing thing nightly — "TONIGHT: THE STAR THAT MOVES!" — and makes two hundred people chant a measurement like a war-cry. Veritan visitors physically flinch, then edge closer.
- **Pallo & Verse** *(Sophara argued + Luminar performed)* — debate-clowns: two fools formally dispute an absurd proposition (*"Resolved: soup is a hat"*), full Colloquy procedure, and the crowd renders a Verdict by roar. Sopharans laugh until they cry and go very quiet on the walk home.
- **The carousel** — Ondrel Vasht's wonder-work: a Forgeholm engine turning painted animals from five kingdoms, and a standing tradition that the gravest person present must ride it. The seats are never quite the same animals twice, and children swear there's one nobody else can see. *(Six seats to a ring. Count them yourself.)*
- **Brindle's round** — the route-caller, after supper, singing the circuit: every town on the Round in order, verse by verse, crowd joining on their own town's line. The only complete map of the world, performed nightly, and nobody thinks of it as a map.
- **The lamp-walk** — **Tullo**, the old lamp-mender, closes the open hours by setting a dozen paper lanterns onto the wind, each named for someone a fairgoer lost this year. He never charges. *(He is the lamp-mender taken after the Kaldrum fair — [the Stutter](../../quests/active/the-stutter.md) — the man who tends lights, taken by the men who put them out. Let the party love him first.)*
- **The rigging display** — **Liss** and **Corb**, the fair's best riggers, race each other up the mast-lines at midnight to re-hang the high lanterns, trading insults the whole way. Fairgoers bet grain-tokens on them. *(The two riggers taken after Kaldrum — [Liss resurfaces at the Foundation](../../locations/the-foundation.md#people-here), still keeping the count.)*

## Food & drink *(read these aloud)*

- **Both-banks bread** *(Sophara)* — two doughs braided and baked as one loaf; the seller will not tell you which strand is which bank's, "because then you'd only eat half."
- **Slag-sugar** *(Forgeholm)* — molten sugar poured on a cold iron slab and shattered with a tiny hammer *you get to swing*; sold in paper cones, tastes of caramel and, faintly, thunderstorms.
- **The long-table stew** *(Agoran)* — the supper pot that never empties and never tastes quite the same twice, because every family adds something; your bowl always contains one ingredient you'd swear came from home.
- **Painted cakes** *(Luminar)* — small cakes glazed like miniature masterpieces: a storm at sea, a city at dusk. Fairgoers hold them up, grieve aloud — *"I can't possibly"* — and eat them in two bites. This is correct and expected.
- **The exact pie** *(Veritas)* — cut into slices so precisely equal that engineers weep; served with the vendor's standing challenge to find the heavy slice (there is never a heavy slice; a Veritan once measured for three hours).
- **Star-water** *(Veritas)* — clear cold spirit served in a black cup "so you can see what the sky drinks." Two-cup limit, enforced by grandmothers.
- **Fair-bread** — the festival six-grain loaf, baked at every stop. Ask a Wayfarer to name the grains and they'll give you five — wheat, rye, barley, oat, millet — "and the old grain," which has no other name, grows only where the wagons camp, and is the reason no other bread ever tastes like the fair. *(GM: the sixth grain is amaranth. Nobody in the world can hear the word. On the sheet, it's one more Guest custom, alive in the bread.)*
- **The last cup** — at the lullaby, ale is served in double-handled cups meant to be shared with a stranger. Keeping one to yourself isn't forbidden. It's just *noticed*.

## Prizes *(d12 — the shelf)*

1. A tin whistle that plays the first bar of the lullaby true, and goes breathy on the rest
2. A troupe-scarf in the fair's colors *(GM: the fair's colors are a **claim** — [worth more than the winner knows](the-wayfair.md#gm-notes))*
3. A carved wooden animal that doesn't match any beast on the carousel — "the spare"
4. A paper lantern from Tullo's stock, your name added to the mantle in grease-pencil
5. A jar of slag-sugar shards and a thumb-sized hammer
6. A "map" of the Round drawn by Brindle — no distances, no compass, songs' first lines as roads. *(It works. No one believes it works.)*
7. A both-banks loaf, still warm
8. A mirror-fragment from Seralind's stage that shows you a half-second late
9. A grain-token good for one supper "at any table of ours, any year"
10. A key that fits no lock the winner will find for a long time *(GM's toy — decide never or perfectly)*
11. A ribbon faded to no particular color, from the sixth wagon's old canopy *(the barkers don't remember stocking it, and won't take it back)*
12. Choose from the shelf — and the barker throws in the story of how the prize came to the fair, which is worth more

## Running the fair — GM kit

- **Sensory loop** (rotate as scene-dressing): woodsmoke and caramel; lantern-light on painted canvas; the carousel's engine purring under Ondrel's whistle; six hundred voices finding one song; underfoot, cinders — this is the Cinderfield, and Kaldrum's hum is *absent* out here, which Kaldrumers describe as "too quiet to sleep."
- **The Loosening, staged:** early night — colors warmer, strangers easy; mid-night — Sopharans *deciding* things, Veritans explaining things to strangers with shining eyes; peak (act 4) — [Wrena's consecration takes](../../characters/npcs/wrena-calder.md#gm-notes), Seralind's painting won't fade, and any PC braid should feel *assisted*. Be generous with Hope on fairgrounds; describe Fear spends as feeling far away ([table guidance](the-wayfair.md#gm-notes)).
- **The Grey After** (morning-after kit): cold firepits, wagons smaller-looking by daylight, everyone's town clothes suddenly drab, the lullaby just out of reach — and [the Gleaner](../../characters/npcs/the-gleaner.md), somewhere along the road home, being kind to someone the fair lit up and set down again. No mechanics; pure scene frame, and her hunting ground.
- **Crowds as terrain:** in any fairground scene, the crowd is an environment — it hides PCs who move with it, slows anyone who shoves against it, and *turns* on whoever draws a blade at the fair (the one thing that breaks its warmth instantly).
- **The empty bunks:** if running [the Stutter](../../quests/active/the-stutter.md), the party should meet Tullo, Liss, and Corb *before* the skip. The frame-up's "fair-folk fled in the night" line should have faces.
