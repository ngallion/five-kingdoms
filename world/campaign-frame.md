# Five Kingdoms — Campaign Frame

***Five kingdoms, each built on one aspect of humanity and starving for the other four — and a king whose every offer is exactly what you're missing.***

**Nature:** The campaign frame — the table-facing synthesis of the whole setting: pitch, tone, character guidance, principles, mechanics, session zero
**Public knowledge:** Everything above the GM Notes is player-safe and checked against [canon.md](canon.md). The player edition lives at [handouts/campaign-primer.md](../handouts/campaign-primer.md)
**See also:** [Overview](overview.md) · [Canon](canon.md) · [Magic](magic.md) · [The Hollowing](the-hollowing.md) · [The Wayfair](factions/the-wayfair.md) · [The Stutter](../quests/active/the-stutter.md)

**COMPLEXITY RATING:** ●●● *(core Daggerheart plus a light Hope mechanic for cross-aspect cooperation, an isolation-themed use of Fear, and Vael/Korrven's layered isolation mechanics — see Campaign Mechanics and GM Notes)*

## THE PITCH

Read this to your players:

> *You come from kingdoms that haven't spoken in two hundred years — five realms ringing a wilderness no map agrees on, each founded on one aspect of humanity and quietly starving for the other four. Now a king has risen in the Kingdom of Community, and his conquest doesn't march: it **arrives**, warm and helpful, offering every kingdom exactly what it lacks. You are the wrong people from the wrong kingdoms, thrown together at a festival on the night something impossible happens — and the only power he has no answer for is people like you learning to need each other.*

## TONE & FEEL

Hopeful, Melancholic, Wondrous, Warm, Uncanny, Tense

The default register is warmth against a cold world: firelight, festivals, and found company, played against long roads, sealed gates, and things in the fog that wear absence. Horror stays at the edges; the center of every scene is what people can be to each other.

## THEMES

- The cost of isolation and insularity
- Strength through interdependence — no single aspect of humanity is enough alone
- How tyrants exploit loneliness: the offer that genuinely helps is the trap
- Home — losing it, carrying it, building it out of the people beside you

## TOUCHSTONES

*Suggestions — swap freely for what your table knows:*

- **Avatar: The Last Airbender** — divided nations, a party drawn from all of them, unity as the win condition
- **The Dark Crystal** — a sundered world, something vital split and drained, healing as the endgame
- **Station Eleven** — a traveling troupe keeping the connected world alive after everything else stopped moving
- **The Night Circus** — the fair that arrives like magic and leaves an ache behind
- **Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood** — warm found-family adventure over machinery that runs on a terrible price

## OVERVIEW

*Player-safe. Share before character creation.*

The old charts call the continent **the Concord** and draw it as a wheel: five kingdoms around the rim, a ring road — **the Girdle** — linking their capitals, and great spoke-roads running inward toward the center. The spoke-roads are still there, superbly built and completely dead: every one stops at the edge of the **Hollow March**, the wild country in the middle of the world that no two maps agree on and nobody visits. The border-gates on the Girdle have been sealed for generations, by guards enforcing a closure none of them can explain.

Each kingdom was founded on one aspect of humanity, and each has spent two centuries mastering its own while forgetting the others exist. **Luminar** makes endless wonders it can never finish or say the point of. **Veritas** knows more than any civilization in history and shares none of it. **Forgeholm** builds machines that work perfectly and can no longer say what they're *for*. **Sophara** has weighed every question and decided nothing in living memory. **Agoran** kept the long tables and the harvest songs — and was so hungry for purpose that five years ago it welcomed a king who supplied one. Each kingdom worships its own god as chief of the pantheon, and no god, anywhere, has answered a prayer in two hundred years.

Two things still move between the kingdoms. The first is **the Wayfair**: a festival-caravan that raises its fair outside a different capital every solstice, waved through sealed gates by guards who afterward can't say why — the only news, letters, and wonder that reliably cross borders, and the closest thing the world has to a shared holiday. The second is newer and worse: **the Hollowing.** The wilds are rising. For five years the things of the border country — things that imitate lost voices and lit windows, things that take the straggler and the cut-off — have multiplied, closing roads and darkening waystations. And into that fear ride the grey-cloaked **King's Wardens** out of Agoran, to any village's aid, any kingdom's, no fee asked. Their motto: *no one stands alone.* The protection is real. So, slowly, gently, is the price — a garrison here, a beacon-line there, and every grateful town bound a little closer to the king who sent help when no one else came.

The king's name is **Korrven**, and he is not a monster out of the old tales — he is worse, because his gifts work. He finds what a kingdom lacks and supplies it; he finds what a person aches for and offers it, and the offer is real. Agoran fell without a battle. His provisioners and wardens are already working the roads toward **Forgeholm**, whose machines feed half the world and whose Guild is very proud and very tired.

The heroes' task is not just to defeat a king. It is to help five kingdoms remember what they need from each other — starting with a handful of strangers from different kingdoms who, against everything their homelands taught them, become one company.

## COMMUNITIES

*All nine communities exist here. Kingdoms are cultures, not bloodlines — any community can hail from any kingdom, but each has natural homes. Pick one or two session zero questions per player.*

#### HIGHBORNE

The capitals' elites: the Luminary circles of Prismere, the Bench families of Kaldrum, the priority-holders of Meridian's Assay. Highborne characters grew up inside their kingdom's aspect at its most rarefied — and closest to the machinery of what it costs.

> *What did your family's standing cost someone you knew by name?*
>
> *What rule of your kingdom's elite did you break, and who saw?*

#### LOREBORNE

Most at home in **Veritas**, where scholarship is the state, or in Sophara's schools. A Loreborne character learned early that in Veritas, knowledge is *kept* — taught to few, shown to none.

> *What do you know that you have never been allowed to share — and who taught it to you at what cost to themselves?*
>
> *What question sent you out through a sealed border?*

#### ORDERBORNE

The disciplined collectives: Forgeholm's Guild floors, Sophara's schools of deliberation, Agoran's warden companies. Orderborne characters know the strength of ranks — and what ranks close around.

> *What did your order refuse to see, and when did you stop pretending not to see it?*
>
> *Whose place in the ranks did you take, or lose?*

#### RIDGEBORNE

The Ironspine is theirs: Forgeholm's high valleys, mining terraces, and cable-lift stations, where winter is a siege and the mountain provides everything except company.

> *Who didn't come back off the mountain, and what do you carry of theirs?*
>
> *What does Forgeholm's machine-fed plenty owe the valleys that feed the machines?*

#### SEABORNE

The world has one coast — Luminar's **Glassreach** — so every Seaborne character shares it: fisherfolk, bargemen, lagoon-town families, the only steady people in a kingdom of beautiful beginnings. Nobody sails out past sight of the cliffs. Nobody ever has.

> *What have you seen in the western water that you've never told a painter?*
>
> *Why does your family hug the coast — superstition, orders, or something that happened?*

#### SLYBORNE

The sorted world's leftovers: Kaldrum's **Unmarked**, the Stoa's **Provisional**, the smugglers and crossing-guides of the border wilds — everyone whose kingdom had no shelf for them. Slyborne characters already know what the kingdoms are only starting to learn: the official world is not the whole world.

> *Which kingdom declared you surplus, and what did you take with you when you left?*
>
> *Who crosses borders because of you, and what do you charge?*

#### UNDERBORNE

Kaldrum is built *into* its mountain — workshop terraces descending to depths where status grows with every level down — and Meridian's archive runs deeper below ground than its towers rise above it. Underborne characters come from the world beneath the kingdoms' floors.

> *What did you find in the deep levels that you were told to forget?*
>
> *What does the surface get wrong about the deep — and what does the deep get wrong about you?*

#### WANDERBORNE

**The Wayfair** — born on the circuit, raised between kingdoms, one of the only people alive with friends on five sides of five walls. A Wanderborne PC starts with what the rest of the party will spend the campaign learning.

> *What's your job when the fair raises itself, and who taught it to you?*
>
> *You've watched every kingdom from the fairground's edge. Which one breaks your heart, and why?*
>
> *Someone left the circuit and settled. Who, where, and do you forgive them?*

#### WILDBORNE

The border wilds — the Thornfell's briar-moors, the Sighing Fens, the grass-steppe of the Hush — belong to no kingdom, which makes them the only places nobody is watching. Wildborne characters grew up in the between, and the between is getting worse.

> *The wilds have been rising for five years. What was the first sign you saw, and who didn't believe you?*
>
> *What does your community know about traveling safely that the kingdoms would pay for — and never ask?*

## ANCESTRIES

All ancestries, in every kingdom. The Five Kingdoms sort people by *aspect*, not blood: a clank raised at a Sopharan debate-garden is Sopharan; a faun family working Kaldrum's terraces has held Guild marks for six generations. Ancestry is texture and personal history; kingdom and community are identity. No ancestry carries mechanical or cultural restrictions here.

## CLASSES

*All classes fit. What changes is what your magic and training are called at home — each kingdom keeps exactly one narrow tradition and assumes it is all magic ever was.*

#### BARDS, ROGUES & THE GRACEFUL

Grace reads as Luminar's **Brightwork** — performance, glamour, presence, the held breath. A bard from anywhere else is stranger and rarer: an Agoran troubadour's Grace is table-song; a Veritan's is a lecture that people inexplicably *feel*. Rogues' Midnight has no kingdom — secrecy and absence are the weather of the whole age.

#### WIZARDS & THE LEARNED

Codex is Veritas's **Exact Art** — silent glyphwork, scrying, magic as notation — traditionally worked alone and never taught. A wizard who *shares* is already a heretic of the good kind. Sophara's **Considered Art** supplies the world's finest wards and cannot strike — a Sopharan caster raised on it has never seen magic land a decisive blow.

#### WARRIORS, GUARDIANS & THE FORGED

Blade and Bone read as Forgeholm's discipline — the body as certified instrument, the edge-litanies of **Runework** — or as Agoran's shared labor and warden training. Valor is Sophara's lost inheritance: resolve *enacted*, the verdict delivered — everything the schools now call philosophically incoherent.

#### DRUIDS, RANGERS & THE WILD

Sage is the Granary's magic — growth, harvest, the living land — and the craft of the border wilds, where the kingdoms' grip thins and all strands answer a little. Rangers and druids are most at home in the between-places, which is exactly where this campaign lives.

#### SERAPHS, CLERICS & THE FAITHFUL

Every kingdom's god has been silent for two hundred years — and divine magic *still works*. A seraph channels their god's aspect directly and feels the silence personally: prayer like a well-kept pump over a well no one has heard water in for two centuries. If your god ever answers, it will matter enormously. **Splendor is the setting's open mystery:** healing belongs to no kingdom, no tradition, and no temple — every kingdom has healers, and not one can say where the craft comes from.

#### SORCERERS & THE UNSORTED

Arcana is the oldest magic — the raw strands, wild and pre-tradition. Sorcerers fit no kingdom's school, which in a sorted world is its own story: were you called blessed, broken, or dangerous at home?

## PLAYER PRINCIPLES

#### CARRY YOUR KINGDOM WITH YOU

Play your homeland's aspect *and* its hunger. A Veritan knows things and struggles to share them; an Agoran trusts the group and aches for a purpose. Your kingdom's virtue and its wound both live in you — let both show.

#### NAME WHAT YOU'RE MISSING

Every character in this world lacks something their homeland could not give them. Decide what yours is, say it out loud at session zero, and let it be visible in play. Vulnerability is not a weakness here — it is the campaign's currency, and the villain's; better your party finds your wound before he does.

#### BRAID, DON'T SOLO

The game rewards combining what your characters are: a plan argued one way and executed another, art aimed by knowledge, craft guided by wisdom. When you're stuck, the answer is usually another player's sheet. Reach for each other on purpose, and often.

#### CHOOSE COMPANY

Isolation is the enemy's terrain — walking off alone, keeping the secret, nursing the grudge. You'll do these things anyway, because they're human; the principle is to *notice*, and to let your character be talked back to the fire. Coming back is the heroic act.

## GM PRINCIPLES

#### THE OFFER IS ALWAYS REAL

Korrven and his agents never lie about the gift — the protection protects, the contract pays, the belonging warms. Play every offer with a straight face and genuine value, and let the price live in the fine print and the years ahead. If the players are never tempted, the theme isn't landing.

#### REVEAL THROUGH SYMPTOMS

This world runs on hidden machinery, and none of it should be explained before it is *felt*. Show the stutter, the hole in each kingdom's craft, the silence in the sanctum, the habit no one can source — and let players assemble the pattern. When they theorize at the table, you are doing it right.

#### COMPANY IS ARMOR, ISOLATION IS PREY

The world's dangers orient toward whoever stands alone — mechanically, narratively, socially. Threaten the isolated, hesitate before the united, and make both visible, so the players learn the physics of their world by watching it work.

#### MAKE THE WORLD A SCOREBOARD

Where the party heals, show it: a waystation relit, flocks back on a hillside, a stranger humming. Where they haven't yet been, let it worsen. The map should read as a running tally of what their method — connection — actually does.

## DISTINCTIONS

#### THE FIVE KINGDOMS AND THEIR LACKS

| Kingdom | Aspect | What they have | What they lack |
|---------|--------|----------------|----------------|
| **Luminar** | Creativity | Boundless invention, art, expression | Purpose — creation for its own sake |
| **Veritas** | Science | Deep understanding of the world | Community to share discovery |
| **Forgeholm** | Technology | Tools, engineering, industry | Wisdom to guide it |
| **Sophara** | Philosophy | Wisdom, ethics, deep inquiry | Action — thought become paralysis |
| **Agoran** | Community | Belonging, solidarity, shared labor | Meaning — togetherness without purpose |

#### THE WHEEL AND THE HOLLOW MARCH

The continent faces inward, toward a place nobody goes. Dead six-lane spoke-roads run from every capital to the edge of the March and stop; the Girdle rings the rim through five sealed border-forts. Crossing the world means going *around* it — weeks by wagon — while the dead roads through the center would take days. When players ask why nobody takes them, that's the point.

#### THE HOLLOWING

The wilds are rising, and the things in them wear absence — a voice you last heard from someone gone, a lit window where no house stands. They take the straggler, the feuding village, the cut-off; survivors all learn the same rule, the one people know without being able to say aloud: **company is armor.** The grey-cloaked King's Wardens are the only help that ever comes, and their help is real. Children in every kingdom are scared with the same story under different names — the Hollow One, who cannot open a door itself, and is drawn to the child who sulks alone.

#### THE WAYFAIR AND THE LOOSENING

Every solstice, the fair rises outside a different capital: five ceremonial wagons, a sixth kept painted and empty "for the guest," and one law — no performance uses one aspect alone. While the fair stands, something lifts: strangers talk easily, Sopharans *decide* things, and people feel a homesickness for a place they've never been. It fades in days. Festival-goers call the come-down *fair-ache* and decide it was drink and lanternlight.

#### THE FIVE INSTRUMENTS

Each capital has an involuntary instrument no visitor forgets and no local hears anymore: Kaldrum's hum under the streets, the Stoa's mirror-still river, Meridian's noon bell, the tide that applauds in Prismere, Commonhold's leaning flame. Locals will tell you every city has its quirk. Nobody has ever compared notes.

#### THE SILENCE OF THE GODS

Each kingdom worships its own god as chief of a pantheon no two kingdoms describe the same way — and no god has answered in 214 years. Divine magic still works; the clergy have quietly stopped expecting anything on the other end of prayer. The ones who haven't stopped are the interesting ones. And in every kingdom, people hum the same lullaby none of them can source — and all of them are missing a verse.

## THE INCITING INCIDENT

Midsummer solstice, outside the forge-city of **Kaldrum**. The Wayfair has raised its fair on the Cinderfield — its familiar, beloved turn on the circuit — and the party, drawn from different kingdoms, is on the fairground the night the festival peaks. Then the city's one constant changes: **the Mother Engine stutters.** Output drops across all of Kaldrum; forty thousand people wake like a sleeper whose heart skipped. By morning the Guild wants a culprit, the king's provisioner has one ready — the fair — and "protection" besides, and the party is standing at the exact intersection of the fair, the Guild, and the king's opening move.

Full arc, clues, encounters, and outcome ladder: **[The Stutter](../quests/active/the-stutter.md)**. Four on-ramps for getting the party inside it are written there — pick one or stack several *at session zero, once the party's wounds are known*.

## CAMPAIGN MECHANICS

*Codified from the lore files; each links to its source. Nothing here changes core rules — these are standing rulings on Hope, Fear, and adversary behavior.*

#### THE BRAID

When two or more PCs deliberately combine **different aspects** toward one goal — a working shaped by one and aimed by another's knowledge, a plan argued in Sopharan terms and executed with Forgeholm hands — lean generous with Hope: grant a Hope to the participants, or improve the result a step. Once per scene is plenty. The party is a small traveling version of what the fair does. → [Magic — the Braid](magic.md#at-the-daggerheart-table)

#### COMPANY IS ARMOR

March-things spotlight whoever has no ally in Close range, and their Fear moves *separate* — a mimicked voice, a doused light, one PC called into the dark. A party acting in genuine concert makes them hesitate: describe recoil at laughter, at song, at a braided act. A party that fractures gets hunted. → [The Hollowing — at the table](the-hollowing.md#gm-notes)

#### THE LOOSENING

On Wayfair fairgrounds while the fair stands, be freer still with Hope for cross-aspect cooperation, and describe Fear spends as feeling *harder to reach*. The **Grey After** — the days after the fair departs — is the reverse: a soft, melancholy scene frame, and prime ground for recruiters who sell belonging. → [The Wayfair — table notes](factions/the-wayfair.md#gm-notes)

#### FEAR IS THE WILDS

When you bank Fear, it's the wilds pressing in; when you spend it, something on the road has noticed the party. Say so out loud sometimes — the players should come to feel the Fear pool as the world's loneliness gathering weight. → [The Hollowing — at the table](the-hollowing.md#gm-notes)

## SESSION ZERO QUESTIONS

*Ask a few of these of every player, beyond the community questions above.*

> *Which kingdom raised you — and what did it give you that you're proud of?*
>
> *What did it fail to give you? (This is your wound. The table should know it, because the villain will.)*
>
> *Why were you at the solstice fair outside Kaldrum — trade, flight, curiosity, or something you don't talk about?*
>
> *You've met someone from another kingdom before, which almost never happens. Who, and what did they leave you with?*
>
> *Everyone knows the lullaby. When did you last sing it, and for whom?*
>
> *What does home smell like — and would they take you back?*
>
> *What's the kindest thing a stranger ever did for you? The cruelest?*

**Session zero procedure.** Collect each PC's **wound** (question two) verbatim — it is the campaign's most load-bearing player input ([PLAN §2 #10](../PLAN.md)). File one PC file per player under `characters/pcs/`, wound in its GM Notes. Then pick the on-ramp(s) from [the Stutter](../quests/active/the-stutter.md#hook) to fit: framed PCs suit wounds about belonging/reputation; the Sera ask suits wounds about being needed; the perception on-ramp suits seeker wounds; the taking suits wounds about losing people. Krane will later weaponize the wounds as manufactured motives — write her frame-up *from this list*.

**Vael at the table — DECIDED** *(resolves the open PLAN item "how does Vael work on the party?"; all three drafted options are combined into one layered system rather than picked between)*:

- **Thin Magic** *(passive, always live)*: under Vael's attention, magic goes lonely. A PC with no ally in Close range pays a Hope to attempt a spellcast, or takes disadvantage (pick one house rule and hold it steady all campaign), and the Braid cannot form between PCs who aren't within Close range of each other. Anti-magic in this setting is always, recognizably, isolation ([magic.md — GM Notes](magic.md#gm-notes)).
- **The Whisper** *(active Fear move)*: usable against a PC with no ally in Close range, in any scene where Vael's attention rests on the party. Vael speaks *in the PC's own inner voice* — never commands, only doubts. The player either answers a private question from you ("what do you distrust about the party right now?" — true until they say it aloud to the others) or marks a Stress.
- **The Unraveling clock** *(campaign-scale intensifier)*: tick it whenever an arc ends with the party split by genuine unresolved conflict; an on-screen reconciliation clears a tick. At each threshold, Thin Magic's cost and the Whisper's frequency both step up — Vael's grip visibly tightens the longer the party stays fractured, and visibly loosens after a real reconciliation scene. This is the throughline that ties the other two together: they are always active, and the clock is how hard they bite.

Full at-the-table detail (thresholds, GM cues): [vael-the-unmade.md — At the Daggerheart Table](vael-the-unmade.md#at-the-daggerheart-table).

**Korrven's signature moves — DECIDED** *(resolves the open PLAN item "design his signature move early"; all three drafted options are combined, not picked between)*:

- **Alone Among You** *(passive trait, always live)*: his effectiveness scales with the party's cohesion — for each PC without an ally in Close range, his attacks gain a bonus or his Fear moves cost less; at full company he is markedly, visibly weaker. This is the campaign's thesis, legible in the final fight's geometry.
- **The Offer, Perfected** *(Fear-spend move, once per PC per confrontation)*: he spends Fear to name that PC's wound aloud — drawn straight from the session-zero wound list — and offers to fill it. The player either engages in character (marking a Stress as the words land) or takes the gift (gain 2 Hope now; Korrven banks 2 Fear and the fiction remembers). Foreshadowed by Krane's Act 1 frame-up in [the Stutter](../quests/active/the-stutter.md).
- **Sever the Braid** *(Fear-spend move)*: he spends Fear to make the Braid impossible for a round — strands refusing each other in his presence — counterable only by a PC spending Hope *for someone else*. He reaches for this specifically against parties who lean on the Braid as their identity.

He has all three tools and picks between the two Fear-spend moves scene by scene; the passive trait runs underneath both. Full stat block: [the-king.md — Daggerheart Stats](../characters/npcs/the-king.md#daggerheart-stats).
