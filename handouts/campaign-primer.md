# Five Kingdoms — Player Primer

*Player-safe. Everything here can be read, shared, and speculated about freely.*

## The Pitch

You come from kingdoms that haven't spoken in two hundred years — five realms ringing a wilderness no map agrees on, each founded on one aspect of humanity and quietly starving for the other four. Now a king has risen in the Kingdom of Community, and his conquest doesn't march: it **arrives**, warm and helpful, offering every kingdom exactly what it lacks. You are the wrong people from the wrong kingdoms, thrown together at a festival on the night something impossible happens — and the only power he has no answer for is people like you learning to need each other.

**Tone:** hopeful, melancholic, wondrous, warm, uncanny, tense — firelight and found company against long roads and sealed gates.

**Touchstones:** *Avatar: The Last Airbender*, *The Dark Crystal*, *Station Eleven*, *The Night Circus*, *Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood*.

## The Five Kingdoms

| Kingdom | Aspect | What they have | What they lack |
|---------|--------|----------------|----------------|
| **Luminar** | Creativity | Boundless invention, art, expression | Purpose — creation for its own sake |
| **Veritas** | Science | Deep understanding of the world | Community to share discovery |
| **Forgeholm** | Technology | Tools, engineering, industry | Wisdom to guide it |
| **Sophara** | Philosophy | Wisdom, ethics, deep inquiry | Action — thought become paralysis |
| **Agoran** | Community | Belonging, solidarity, shared labor | Meaning — togetherness without purpose |

**Luminar** holds the world's only coast — the Glassreach, whose light painters ruin themselves chasing. Its capital, **Prismere**, rises from a drowned river-mouth, every surface painted and repainted before it can become familiar. **Veritas** is a high, clear-skied plateau; its capital, **Meridian**, is a grid of domes and instrument-towers whose archive runs deeper below ground than the towers rise above it. **Forgeholm** is the mountain north — the Ironspine — and its forge-capital **Kaldrum** is built *into* a peak, workshop terraces descending toward the great engine at its root. **Sophara** is the gentle river country of the south; its capital, **the Stoa**, is a thousand linked courtyards and debate-gardens where which branch of the river is the *true* river has been formally disputed for four hundred years. **Agoran** is the Granary — big-sky plains where no household survives winter alone; its capital, **Commonhold**, is a hundred villages grown together around the old moot-ring. Five years ago, Agoran welcomed a king. His name is **Korrven**, his banners fly from the ring-barns, and his wardens now patrol roads far beyond his borders.

## The Shape of the World

The old charts draw the continent as a wheel: five kingdoms around the rim, the **Girdle** road linking their capitals, and great spoke-roads running inward — six lanes wide, superbly built, and completely dead. Every spoke stops at the edge of the **Hollow March**, the wild country at the center of the world that no two maps agree on and nobody visits. The border-gates on the Girdle have been sealed for generations, manned by guards enforcing a closure none of them can explain. Crossing the world means going around it, for weeks. The dead roads through the middle would take days. Nobody takes them.

## The Roads Are Dying

Folk call it **the Hollowing**. For a generation the border wilds have worsened; for the last five years the worsening has had teeth — caravans waiting for escorts that don't come, waystations standing dark, villages that lost nobody in living memory starting to lose people. The things out there wear absence: a voice that answers in the fog with words you last heard from someone gone, a lit window where no house stands. They take the straggler, the feuding village, the cut-off. Every survivor learns the same rule, the one people know without being able to say aloud: **company is armor.** Three friends singing come home; a bickering caravan of twenty doesn't.

Into that fear ride the grey-cloaked **King's Wardens** out of Agoran — to any settlement's plea, any kingdom's, no fee, no questions. Their motto is stitched on every cloak: *no one stands alone.* They are the finest fighters of the things alive, and in a hundred villages across four kingdoms they are the only neighbor who ever came. The protection is real. So, gently, is what follows it: a garrison, a beacon-line, a warden-post on the road.

Children in every kingdom are scared with the same story under different names — the **Hollow One**, who cannot open a door itself, is drawn to the child who sulks alone, and does not eat you: it eats your *place*, so that no one comes looking. Parents tell it as nonsense.

## The Fair

Twice a year, at each solstice, **the Wayfair** raises its fair outside a different capital — wagons, stages, kitchens, and wonder-works, waved through sealed gates by guards who afterward can't say why. It is the only news, letters, and small trade that still reliably crosses borders, and its people come from every kingdom: anyone the kingdoms had no shelf for. Its one law: no performance uses one aspect alone — art painted to an argument, a discovery shouted to a crowd, an engine running a carousel with a philosopher riding it, laughing.

While the fair stands, something lifts. Strangers are easy to talk to. Sopharans *decide* things. People feel a homesickness for a place they've never been, and some weep without knowing why. Then the fair moves on, and within days it fades — festival-goers call the come-down *fair-ache* and decide the whole thing was drink and lanternlight.

## Gods and Magic

Each kingdom worships its own god as chief of the pantheon — **Ora** the First Spark in Luminar, **Theora** the Clear Eye in Veritas, **Ferrun** the Shaping Hand in Forgeholm, **Sophar** the Weigher in Sophara, **Tavan** the Long Table in Agoran — and no two kingdoms' theologies agree. One thing they share and never advertise: **no god has answered a prayer in two hundred years.** Divine magic still works; the clergy have quietly stopped expecting anything on the other end of it.

Magic is real, rare, and narrow. Each kingdom keeps exactly one tradition — Luminar's glamours, Veritas's silent glyph-work, Forgeholm's runes, Sophara's peerless wards, Agoran's many-handed blessings — and assumes it is all magic ever was. Travelers say stranger things are possible in the border country, where the kingdoms' grip thins. Healers exist everywhere, and — a curiosity nobody examines — healing belongs to no kingdom's tradition at all.

And everyone, in every kingdom, knows the same lullaby. Nobody can say where it came from. Everyone's version is missing a verse.

## Your Character

- **Any ancestry, any kingdom.** The Five Kingdoms sort people by aspect, not blood — ancestry is your texture and family story; your kingdom and community are your identity.
- **Any community.** Highborne capital elites, Loreborne scholars of Meridian, Orderborne guild floors and warden companies, Ridgeborne mountain valleys, Seaborne folk of the world's one coast, Slyborne souls their kingdom had no shelf for, Underborne from beneath the cities' floors, Wanderborne of the fair's circuit, Wildborne of the between-lands.
- **Any class.** Your training just has a home: Grace reads as Luminari stagecraft, Codex as Veritan glyphs, Blade and Bone as Forgeholm discipline or Agoran labor, Valor as the decisiveness Sophara lost, Sage as the Granary's living magic. Seraphs serve gods who don't answer — yet.
- **Come from different kingdoms.** The party works best as strangers from across the wheel — the kind of company the world hasn't seen in two hundred years.

**How to play it:**

- **Carry your kingdom with you** — its virtue *and* its hunger both live in you; let both show.
- **Name what you're missing** — every character lacks something home couldn't give them. Say yours out loud; it's the campaign's currency.
- **Braid, don't solo** — when you're stuck, the answer is usually another player's sheet. Reach for each other on purpose.
- **Choose company** — walking off alone is the enemy's terrain. You'll do it anyway; the heroic act is coming back to the fire.

## Where It Begins

Midsummer solstice, outside the forge-city of Kaldrum. The fair has come, familiar and beloved, and you are on the fairground the night the festival peaks — for your own reasons, which is one of the first things worth deciding.

## Questions Worth Answering Before We Start

> *Which kingdom raised you — and what did it give you that you're proud of?*
>
> *What did it fail to give you?*
>
> *Why were you at the solstice fair outside Kaldrum — trade, flight, curiosity, or something you don't talk about?*
>
> *You've met someone from another kingdom before, which almost never happens. Who, and what did they leave you with?*
>
> *Everyone knows the lullaby. When did you last sing it, and for whom?*
>
> *What does home smell like — and would they take you back?*
